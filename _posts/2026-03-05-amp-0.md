In this series of blogs, I plan to go about the fundamentals of BJT, MOSFET amplifiers.

When I first learned about transistors as amplifiers during my freshman year, the concept never fully clicked for me. I understood the theory well enough to pass exams, but I never truly grasped why such a device was important or how it was used in real systems.

Recently, I’ve started developing a stronger interest in analog electronics  For a while I was more focused on digital systems, but I’ve since decided to shift my attention toward analog design thanks to the robotics team that I am a part of where I am in charge of designing PCBs. I've also worked on a few small projects using 555 timer since we studied it last year.  Learning about amplifiers feels like a good place to start, since they form the foundation of so many analog circuits.

I am also interested in building projects related to audio and biomedical signals (like ECGs). And these fields rely heavily on amplificaltion, signal conditioning and noise considerations. 

I’m also currently mentoring some juniors and guiding them through the process of building their own Class-D amplifier. That experience made me realize that if I want to explain these ideas clearly to others, I need to deepen my own understanding first.

This blog series is my way of documenting that journey. The goal is simple: to learn amplifier concepts properly and share what I discover with other electronics enthusiasts along the way.
