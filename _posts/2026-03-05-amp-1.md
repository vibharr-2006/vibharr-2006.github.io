## What is an amplifier?

<p>An amplifer is a device that takes in an input signal and gives out a signal with an increased magnitude. For instance consider a microphone, the input to the mic is our speech signal which is a few millivolts in magnitude. The mic has an inbuilt amplifier which raises the magnitude of speech signal which is hen ta=ransmitted via an antenna to the rest of the room. In this article we're going to undertand the priciple and structure of the amplifier, specifically a voltage amplifier.</p>

## A very brief intro to Diodes

<p>I won't get into the physics behind the working of a diode. All you need to know for now is that a diode consists of two currents: diffusion and drift current.</p>

<ul>
    <li>Diffusion current: movement of majority charged carriers due to the concetration gradient across the junction.</li>
    <li>Drift current: movement of minorit charged carriers due to electric field at the junction.</li>
</ul>

<p>In forward bias, diffusion current is greater than drift current. Current starts flowing the moment the voltage across the diode is greater than the threshold voltage</p>
<p>In reverse bias, drift current is greater than diffusion. Very little current flows across the diode. </p>


## A not very brief intro to BJTs
<p>A BJT consists of a collector (n-type), base (p-type) and emitter(n-type). The emitter is heavily doped with electrons, base is lightly doped and very thin and the collector is normally doped. One voltage is applied at the base. Another voltage is appled across the collector and emitter. To turn on the transistor, Vbe (potential difference between base and emitter) must be greater than or equal to 0.7V.</p>

<p>There are mainly three modes of operation for a BJT: cuttof, saturation and active region. </p>
