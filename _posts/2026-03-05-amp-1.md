## What is an amplifier?

<p>An amplifer is a device that takes in an input signal and gives out a signal with an increased magnitude. For instance consider a microphone, the input to the mic is our speech signal which is a few millivolts in magnitude. The mic has an inbuilt amplifier which raises the magnitude of speech signal which is hen ta=ransmitted via an antenna to the rest of the room. In this article we're going to undertand the priciple and structure of the amplifier, specifically a voltage amplifier.</p>

## Operation of a voltage amplifier
    h
    
<p>    A voltage amplifier is a voltage dependent current source. Here, the current source depends on the input voltage (I = k * Vin). This current 
passes through a load resistor through which the output voltage can be measured. </p>

                                                Vout = Rl * I
                                                     = Rl * K * Vin

From the formula above, we can tell that the input voltage gets amplified. 
But what device acts as an voltage dependent current source? This is where a BJT comes in handy. 

## Structure of BJT

<p> In an NPN transistor, one p-type semiconductor is sandwiched between two n-type semiconductors and they are labeled as emitter (n-type), base (p-type) and collector (n-type). </p>

<p> The emitter is heavily doped with electrons, base is lightly doped and very thin and the collector is the largest amoung the three. </p>
